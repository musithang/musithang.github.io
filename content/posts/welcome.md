You somehow found your way here. Either you were looking for something else, or my SEO is better than I thought. Probably the former.

I have 1,847 notes in an Obsidian vault. Four years of them, give or take. Every problem I have ever solved is in there somewhere.

That is not a boast. That is the problem.

Last week I went looking for something I knew I had written down. I was certain about it. I could picture the note. Here is what searching my own vault actually looks like:

```
"why does this work"        47 results

daily/2024-02-18.md         IT WAS THE POWER SUPPLY
2024-09-30.md               this is important
inbox/untitled 6.md         revisit this
rust/notes.md               ok so the fix was obviously
hardware/sdr.md             see other note
```

There is no other note. There has never been another note.

Past-me flagged something as important without mentioning what it was, wrote "revisit this" on a page containing one sentence and a screenshot of a terminal, and then did not revisit it for eighteen months.

Everything is written down. Nothing is findable.

## Storage is not retrieval

That distinction sounds pedantic right up until it costs you an evening.

A pile is storage. You put things in, it accepts everything, judges nothing, and grows quietly in the dark. An archive is retrieval. Its entire purpose is handing something back to you later, in a state where you can still use it.

My vault is excellent storage. As retrieval, it is a haystack with a search box.

The fix is not more tags or a better folder structure. I have tried both, at length, with the enthusiasm of a man avoiding actual work. The fix is that some things have to be finished before they can be found.

## What publishing does to a thought

A note is allowed to trail off. Nobody is waiting for the end of it. You type IT WAS THE POWER SUPPLY at 2 AM and it is completely sufficient, because at that moment you are the only reader and you already know the whole story.

Six months later that sentence is a fossil. Correct, and useless.

Publishing breaks the habit. Not because anyone is out there waiting for it. The readership here is, optimistically, three people and a crawler.

It works because writing for a stranger forces you to include the part you skipped: what you thought was happening, what was actually happening, and why the obvious fix was wrong. That part never survives in a private note. Past-you did not need it explained, and past-you was writing for exactly one person, who already knew.

So that is what this place is for. It is a retrieval system that only accepts finished thoughts, and I am both the librarian and the only guaranteed reader.

## The apparatus

Every entry gets a number and a barcode. This one is MT-001-42FE.

MT-001 is the entry number. 42FE is the front of the title's MD5 hash. Together they make a string I can say out loud, search for, or scan off a printed page, which means an entry here has a handle in a way that a note called `untitled 6` never will.

The barcode is genuine Code 39. The bars decode back to that exact string. Not a picture of a barcode, an actual one. Whether that is useful, or simply the most elaborate thing I have ever done to avoid naming a file properly, is left to the reader.

## The frame is fiction, the writing is not

The catalogue numbers, the status labels, the metadata grids: that part is a costume, and I like it. The internet used to be allowed to look like something, before every page converged on the same rounded rectangle with a cookie banner stapled to the bottom.

The writing underneath it is not part of the costume. The projects are real, the mistakes are real, and when something is uncertain I intend to say so, rather than wrapping it in enough technobabble that it starts sounding authoritative by accident. The barcode is genuine. The confidence is not always earned.

## What ends up here

Whatever survives the process above. Some entries will be long and technical. Some will be a single wrong assumption that took two weeks to notice. Some will have no conclusion at all and get filed anyway, because "this did not work, and here is exactly how far I got" is a legitimate record.

Plenty of it will age badly. In a year there will be an entry here that makes me wince, and it stays, because an archive that keeps only the parts that aged well is a portfolio wearing a lab coat.

Everything is already written down somewhere.

This is the part where I make it findable.
