The previous version ran for three years, and I touched it exactly once: when it broke. That kind of stability is admirable, and a lie. It didn't stay unchanged because it was finished — it stayed unchanged because I was afraid to touch it.

Plans always look better than what gets built — that is not pessimism, it is a unit of measurement. If the finished thing resembles the sketch by seventy percent, the sketch was good. At ninety, I was being too careful.

## The system that asks for nothing

A static site asks the least of me: no database to back up, no update deciding something on my behalf at three in the morning. The content sits in files, the files sit in version control, and version control remembers the parts I would rather forget.

> An archive is not storage for the past. An archive is the place where abandoned ideas stop talking back.

The editor follows the same logic: fields, not possibilities. If something cannot be entered, that usually means it should not have been.

## What I will get wrong next

There will be search, because without it the archive is just a pile. There will be retroactive tagging, which I will abandon after two weeks. And there will be a "reading queue" section whose mere existence is an indictment.
